Claim boundaries
The toggle/predicate theorem quantifies over any State type. Its hypotheses
state that the toggle is involutive and the predicate ignores that toggle.
The integer cube instance derives predicate invariance from bit-mask
exclusion. Ordered involution words have left and right reverse-word inverses.
Signed basis pairs retain Boolean phase and natural label separately. The
second action evaluates its phase at the first action's translated label.
A signed-XOR word fixing label zero acts identically on labels; its phase
can remain nonconstant. The explicit diagonal action preserves every label
and distinguishes the two controlled branches at label one.
The N=21 examples quantify only multipliers 2, 4 and 16 on the identity-padded
five-bit map. The common non-affinity witness uses inputs 8 and 16: their XOR
is 24, the padded output is 24, and the XORs of their outputs are 27, 10 and 6.
The fixed-origin obstruction concerns a single signed-XOR word. LCU sums,
arbitrary reversible circuits and physical coherence have separate contracts.
The fold theorem concerns canonical rational scalar pairs. A general
Cayley-Dickson tower embedding and physical interpretations remain external.
The channel theorems concern one common appended stochastic binary channel.
They require deterministic bounds on all four means and a positive center-gap
margin. Statistical coverage, stationarity, calibration, replacement readout
and context-dependent instruments remain experiment-owned obligations.
