Write phi for the sign exponent and
  g(a,b,x) = phi(b,x) xor phi(a,b xor x) xor phi(a xor b,x) xor phi(a,b).
The new general doubling theorem proves that the three appended high bits
enter g only linearly; every quadratic high-bit term cancels. Its coefficients
are expressed through the lower commutation signs and nonzero predicates.
This is the general associator-lifting mechanism, without a claim to have
formalized the census's subsequent relation-class counts.

For a fixed lower pair a,b, define
  c = N(a) xor N(b) xor N(a xor b).
The proof establishes g'(x,h) = g(x) xor (h and c) for every order and pair.
For distinct nonzero a,b, c=1; zero or repeated pairs have c=0. Consequently
  W(g', (k,eta)) = 2 W(g,k) if eta=c, and 0 otherwise.
Division by 2^(n+1) preserves the lower normalized amplitude exactly; squaring
preserves its ideal sampling weight, with a deterministic appended output c.
The integer Walsh theorem is kernel-checked; the normalization statement is
an algebraic corollary stated here, not a separate Born-law proof.

New deduction within this reconciliation: after a pair first appears,
ambient dimension growth adds deterministic parity coordinates to its ideal
Walsh experiment. It does not increase the lower-coordinate information.
This does not apply to the cross-scale family, whose pair changes with D.
That family approaches an affine function even as support and degree grow:
its retained dominant weight (1-16/D)^2 approaches one. Degree, spectral
concentration, configuration count and hardware cost must therefore be
reported as distinct measures. This audit claims no literature priority.

Readout consequence of exact cancellation
-----------------------------------------
For a fixed lower independent pair, every input x contributes opposite signs
at high bits 0 and 1. An ideal uniform sign average therefore vanishes after
doubling, while the Walsh readout has a deterministic high output bit and
retains the complete lower spectrum. Conditional on the controlled-oracle,
preparation and Born contracts, a vanishing Hadamard-test average can thus
reflect exact algebraic parity cancellation. Such a zero average alone does
not diagnose decoherence or the absence of pointwise associator defects.
This consequence concerns a fixed pair, rather than averaging all triples;
it supplies a matched ideal baseline for choosing the observable.
